How to Trace a Cryptocurrency Transaction

Public blockchains can expose transaction information that helps reconstruct the movement of cryptocurrency. A transaction review is useful for documenting what happened, but it is not the same as proving who controls an address or guaranteeing recovery.

Start with the transaction hash

A transaction hash or transaction ID is usually the best starting point. It can be entered into the appropriate blockchain explorer for the network involved.

Identify the relevant addresses

Record the sending and receiving wallet addresses shown by the transaction. If funds move again, document subsequent transactions and keep the full chain of hashes where possible.

Check the network and asset

Bitcoin, Ethereum, stablecoins, and other assets can operate on different networks. Make sure the transaction is being reviewed on the correct chain and that the asset and network are not being confused.

Build a transaction timeline

Organize transfers chronologically, including amounts, timestamps, addresses, and transaction hashes. A simple timeline can make a complex incident much easier to understand.

Understand the limits

A public address does not necessarily reveal the real-world identity of its controller. Exchange attribution, account records, legal process, or other evidence may be required to connect blockchain activity to a person or organization.

Preserve the source information

Save links, screenshots, exported records, and transaction identifiers used in your review. Do not rely on a single screenshot when the underlying transaction record is available.

Safety reminder: Never provide private keys, seed phrases, passwords, or authentication codes to someone claiming they can recover cryptocurrency. Recovery is never guaranteed.
