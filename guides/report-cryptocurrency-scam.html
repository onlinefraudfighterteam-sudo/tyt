How to Report a Cryptocurrency Scam

If you have lost cryptocurrency to fraud, reporting the scam can create a record of what happened and may help the appropriate organization identify related activity. Reporting does not guarantee that funds will be recovered, but a clear report with useful evidence is more valuable than a general description.

Important: Do not pay anyone who promises guaranteed cryptocurrency recovery. Recovery claims, upfront fees, fake investigators, and people impersonating government agencies can be part of a second scam.

1. Stop further payments and protect your accounts

Stop sending additional cryptocurrency to the suspected scammer. If your exchange, wallet, email, social-media account, or another account may have been compromised, use the provider's official security process to secure it.

Do not use phone numbers, links, or contact details supplied by the suspected scammer. Find the organization's official website independently.

2. Preserve the evidence

Save the original information you have before deleting messages or closing accounts. Useful evidence can include:

  • Cryptocurrency wallet addresses involved.
  • Transaction IDs or transaction hashes.
  • The cryptocurrency type and amount sent.
  • Dates and times of transactions.
  • Exchange, wallet, payment service, or cryptocurrency ATM information.
  • Website addresses, domains, usernames, email addresses, and phone numbers.
  • Emails, text messages, social-media conversations, and other communications.
  • Screenshots of scam websites, accounts, messages, payment instructions, and claimed balances.
  • A timeline showing how contact started, what happened, and when money was sent.

3. Report the transaction to the exchange or service you used

If you sent cryptocurrency through an exchange, wallet provider, payment service, or cryptocurrency ATM, contact that organization through its official support or fraud-reporting channel as soon as possible.

Give them the transaction details and explain that the transfer was connected to fraud. Do not assume an exchange can reverse a blockchain transaction; ask what account-security, fraud-review, or compliance options are available.

4. Report the scam to the appropriate authorities

Reporting options depend on where you live and what happened. Start with your local police or national cybercrime/fraud-reporting authority, and check the official government website for the correct reporting process.

United States: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)

The FBI's IC3 accepts reports of cyber-enabled crime. Its cryptocurrency guidance says useful transaction information can include cryptocurrency addresses, amounts and types of cryptocurrency, transaction hashes, dates and times, communications, and websites involved.

United States: Federal Trade Commission (FTC)

The FTC accepts reports about scams, fraud, and bad business practices through ReportFraud.ftc.gov. The FTC uses reports to help identify fraud patterns, educate the public, and support enforcement.

5. Report the scam website, account, or profile

If the scammer used a website, social-media account, messaging account, advertisement, marketplace listing, or other online service, use that platform's official reporting tools. Include the relevant username, profile URL, website address, advertisement, or message where possible.

6. Report impersonation and fake recovery services

Be especially careful if someone contacts you after the original scam claiming to be a lawyer, investigator, government official, exchange employee, or cryptocurrency recovery specialist.

Do not send additional money or sensitive information simply because someone says they can recover your cryptocurrency. Report suspicious recovery activity through the appropriate platform or authority as well.

7. Organize your report clearly

  1. How you were contacted: website, social media, email, phone, messaging app, or another method.
  2. What you were told: summarize the claims or instructions.
  3. What you sent: cryptocurrency, amount, asset, wallet address, and transaction hash.
  4. Where it went: receiving address, exchange, ATM, platform, or other destination if known.
  5. What evidence you have: screenshots, messages, websites, receipts, and account information.
  6. What happened afterward: withdrawal problems, additional payment demands, account closure, or recovery-scam contact.

8. Keep your report reference numbers

Save confirmation emails, complaint numbers, case numbers, screenshots of submitted forms, and the dates you contacted organizations. These records can make later follow-up easier.

What if you do not have the transaction hash?

Report the scam anyway. Provide as much information as you have, including the wallet address, exchange account, payment receipt, website, username, phone number, email address, and timeline. Missing one piece of information should not stop you from reporting.

What reporting cannot guarantee

A report is not a promise that cryptocurrency will be recovered. Blockchain transactions may be difficult or impossible to reverse, and investigators may need information from multiple services or jurisdictions. Avoid anyone who guarantees recovery or demands an upfront payment to release supposedly recovered funds.

Frequently asked questions

Should I report a cryptocurrency scam if I did not lose money?

Yes. Some authorities accept reports about attempted fraud as well as completed losses. Use the reporting authority's official guidance to determine what information to submit.

Can I report a crypto scam anonymously?

Reporting requirements differ between organizations and countries. Check the official reporting form before submitting information.

Should I contact the scammer to ask for my money back?

Generally, do not continue negotiating with a suspected scammer. Preserve the evidence and use official reporting and account-security channels instead.

Should I pay a recovery company before it investigates?

Be very cautious. A demand for an upfront fee combined with a guarantee of recovery is a major warning sign. Do not send more cryptocurrency simply because someone claims to be able to recover your previous loss.
