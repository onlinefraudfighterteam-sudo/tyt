Romance and Relationship Crypto Scam Guide

Romance and relationship scams can develop into cryptocurrency investment fraud when someone you meet online builds trust and then encourages you to send money, buy cryptocurrency, or use an investment platform they recommend. A relationship, regular communication, photos, video calls, or an apparently successful investment story does not prove that the person or opportunity is genuine.

Important: Do not send cryptocurrency or investment funds to an online love interest you have not independently verified. Be especially cautious when a new relationship quickly turns into investment advice, requests for money, or instructions to use a particular crypto platform.

1. The relationship develops quickly and intensely

A scammer may communicate frequently, give unusually strong attention, or create a sense of emotional closeness before discussing money. Rapid trust-building can make it harder to question later financial requests.

2. The person cannot meet you in person

They may repeatedly explain why they cannot meet, even after making plans. The explanation could involve work, travel, military service, offshore employment, family emergencies, or another story. This alone does not prove fraud, but it becomes more concerning when combined with requests for money or investment.

3. They introduce cryptocurrency or investing

A major warning sign is an online romantic contact who says they can teach you how to invest, has a special trading method, knows an expert, or can guarantee unusually good results. The person may direct you to a website or app that appears professional but is controlled by the scammer.

4. They show you impressive profits

Fake investment platforms can display growing balances, charts, trading histories, or supposed profits. A number shown on a website is not proof that funds exist or that withdrawals are possible.

5. They tell you exactly how and where to send money

Scammers may coach victims through buying cryptocurrency, transferring it to a wallet, wiring money, or using a particular exchange or payment service. Treat detailed payment instructions from someone you met online as a major warning sign.

6. They pressure you to invest more

After showing supposed profits, the person may encourage a larger deposit, claim that an opportunity is limited, or say you need to act quickly. Pressure reduces the time available for independent verification.

7. Withdrawals suddenly require another payment

A common pattern is a demand for a tax, fee, verification deposit, insurance payment, liquidity requirement, penalty, or other charge before supposed profits can be withdrawn. Do not send additional cryptocurrency simply because someone says it will unlock an account.

8. They ask you to keep the relationship or investment secret

A request to hide the relationship, investment, or payments from family, friends, banks, or other trusted people should be treated seriously. Independent advice can make it easier to recognize manipulation.

9. They use a polished identity that is difficult to verify

Scammers can use copied photographs, fabricated employment histories, stolen identities, fake social profiles, and AI-generated content. A convincing profile does not establish that the person is who they claim to be.

10. They move the conversation away from the original platform

A person may quickly ask you to continue on another messaging service, private chat, or encrypted application. Moving platforms is not proof of fraud, but it can make reporting and independent verification more difficult.

11. They discourage independent verification

Be cautious if the person becomes angry when you ask questions, tells you not to search for information, says other people cannot understand the opportunity, or insists that you use only links and contact details they provide.

12. The investment website or app has its own warning signs

Look for guaranteed returns, unusual domains, copied branding, fake regulatory claims, unclear company ownership, unexplained account balances, withdrawal fees, and requests for passwords or wallet secrets. For more detail, see our Fake Investment Website Warning Signs guide.

How to verify before sending money

  1. Pause and do not let emotional pressure make the decision.
  2. Talk with someone you trust who is outside the relationship.
  3. Search independently for the person's name, claimed occupation, profile details, and reported scam patterns.
  4. Consider a reverse-image search of profile photographs.
  5. Verify the investment company and website independently rather than using links supplied by the person.
  6. Check regulatory information through the relevant official regulator or registry where applicable.
  7. Never share passwords, one-time codes, seed phrases, or private keys.
  8. Never pay a new fee simply because a supposed investment platform says a withdrawal is blocked.

If you already sent cryptocurrency

Stop sending additional funds. Preserve the person's profile information, usernames, phone numbers, email addresses, messages, website addresses, screenshots, payment records, wallet addresses, transaction hashes, and dates and times.

If an exchange or payment provider was involved, contact it through an independently verified official channel and explain that the transaction was connected with suspected fraud. You can also report the incident to appropriate authorities in your country.

Be careful of recovery scams

After a loss, another person may claim they can recover your cryptocurrency because they are a hacker, investigator, lawyer, government agent, exchange employee, or recovery specialist. Do not assume such claims are genuine. Be particularly cautious about upfront fees, requests for additional cryptocurrency, passwords, wallet seed phrases, or private keys.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a scam if an online love interest offers to teach me crypto investing?

Treat it as a serious fraud warning. Do not send money or cryptocurrency or use an investment platform recommended by someone you know only online. Verify any investment opportunity independently.

Why do romance scammers talk about cryptocurrency?

Scammers may use cryptocurrency because it can be transferred quickly and because fake investment websites or apps can display convincing but fictitious balances and profits.

What should I do if I already sent cryptocurrency to an online love interest?

Stop sending additional funds, preserve transaction and communication records, contact the exchange or payment provider through an independently verified official channel, and report the suspected fraud to appropriate authorities.
