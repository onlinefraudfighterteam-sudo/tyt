How to Avoid Cryptocurrency Recovery Scams

People who have already lost cryptocurrency are frequently targeted again by people claiming they can recover the money. The safest approach is to treat guaranteed-recovery claims and urgent payment demands as major warning signs.

Be suspicious of guaranteed recovery

No legitimate investigation can guarantee that stolen cryptocurrency will be returned. Be cautious of claims that recovery is certain, immediate, or already approved.

Do not pay to “unlock” your funds

Requests for cryptocurrency, taxes, clearance fees, validation payments, or deposits before a supposed recovery are warning signs. Paying more does not create a guarantee of recovery.

Protect your credentials

A legitimate service should not need your private key or seed phrase to inspect a public blockchain transaction. Never disclose passwords, authentication codes, or recovery phrases to an online contact.

Verify who contacted you

Scammers may impersonate investigators, lawyers, exchanges, government officials, or other organizations. Independently verify contact information rather than using a phone number or link supplied by the person who contacted you.

Preserve the second scam as evidence

Keep emails, chats, invoices, wallet addresses, payment requests, websites, and usernames connected with the attempted recovery scam. This information can be important when reporting the fraud.

Slow down before sending money

Urgency is often used to prevent careful verification. Take time to review the claim, check the organization independently, and understand exactly what is being requested before taking action.

Safety reminder: Never provide private keys, seed phrases, passwords, or authentication codes to someone claiming they can recover cryptocurrency. Recovery is never guaranteed.
