How to Report a Bitcoin Scam

If someone tricked you into sending Bitcoin, reporting the scam can create a useful record of what happened and may help the appropriate organization identify related activity. A report does not guarantee that Bitcoin will be recovered, but precise transaction and communication details can make a report much more useful.

Important: Do not send additional Bitcoin to someone who promises guaranteed recovery. People offering recovery services may target victims after the original scam and demand upfront fees or personal information.

1. Stop further payments and protect your accounts

  • Stop communicating with the scammer if doing so is safe.
  • Do not send another payment because the scammer says it is required to unlock, verify, tax, insure, or recover your Bitcoin.
  • If you shared passwords, authentication codes, or account access, secure those accounts using the official service website or app.
  • If an exchange account may be compromised, contact the exchange through its official support channel.

2. Preserve the Bitcoin transaction information

Bitcoin transactions contain information that can be especially useful when reporting fraud. Preserve the information before deleting messages, closing accounts, or changing devices.

  • Transaction ID / transaction hash: the identifier for the Bitcoin transaction.
  • Recipient Bitcoin address: the address that received the funds, if available.
  • Your sending address: your Bitcoin address, if available.
  • Amount: the amount of Bitcoin sent and, if relevant, the approximate value in your local currency at the time.
  • Date and time: record when the transaction was made, including the time zone if known.
  • Exchange, wallet, or ATM: record the service used to buy, hold, or send the Bitcoin.
  • Receipts and account records: keep purchase records, ATM receipts, withdrawal records, emails, and support tickets.

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) specifically asks cryptocurrency scam victims to provide transaction details such as cryptocurrency addresses, amount and type, date and time, and transaction ID/hash when available.

3. Save evidence about the scammer

Do not rely only on the blockchain transaction. Preserve the story and communications surrounding the payment.

  • Save emails, text messages, chat messages, and relevant social-media conversations.
  • Record the scammer's name, username, phone number, email address, website, domain, and other identifiers.
  • Save screenshots of profiles, websites, investment dashboards, invoices, QR codes, payment instructions, and promises made by the scammer.
  • Write a short timeline explaining how contact began, what the scammer claimed, when you sent Bitcoin, and what happened afterward.
  • Keep original evidence where possible. Make copies for your own records rather than relying on screenshots alone.

4. Contact the Bitcoin exchange, wallet provider, or ATM operator

If you used a cryptocurrency exchange or Bitcoin ATM, contact the provider immediately through contact information you obtain independently from its official website or app.

Tell the provider that the transaction was fraudulent and provide the transaction details. The FTC advises people who paid a scammer with cryptocurrency to contact the cryptocurrency exchange or ATM operator immediately, explain that the transaction was fraudulent, and ask whether the transaction can be reversed or the funds returned.

What to include:
  • Bitcoin transaction ID/hash
  • Sending and receiving addresses
  • Amount of Bitcoin
  • Date and time
  • Your account or customer information, where appropriate
  • Relevant scam communications and supporting evidence

5. Report the Bitcoin scam to the appropriate authorities

United States

If you are in the United States, the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) accepts reports of cryptocurrency scams. The FBI encourages victims to report even when they do not have every transaction detail. You can also report consumer fraud to the Federal Trade Commission.

Official reporting resources:

  • FBI IC3 Cryptocurrency Crime Guidance
  • FBI IC3 Complaint Form
  • FTC ReportFraud

Outside the United States

Reporting procedures vary by country. Consider reporting the fraud to your local police or national cybercrime reporting service, the relevant financial or consumer-protection regulator, and the exchange or platform involved.

Use official government websites to find the correct reporting channel for your country. Do not use a phone number, email address, or website supplied by the scammer.

6. If a Bitcoin ATM was involved

If you sent Bitcoin through a cryptocurrency ATM or kiosk, preserve the printed receipt and any transaction documentation. Record the kiosk operator, location, date and time, and the Bitcoin address or transaction ID shown in your records.

The FBI advises victims of cryptocurrency-kiosk scams to report the incident to local police and to IC3, providing available transaction and kiosk information.

7. Be careful after you report the scam

Reporting a Bitcoin scam can attract a second wave of fraud. Someone may claim to be a blockchain investigator, lawyer, government official, exchange employee, or recovery specialist and say they can retrieve your Bitcoin for a fee.

  • Do not pay an upfront recovery fee simply because someone says they can recover your Bitcoin.
  • Do not give a stranger your wallet seed phrase, private key, passwords, or authentication codes.
  • Do not trust a person merely because they use a government logo, legal-sounding language, or a professional-looking website.
  • Verify organizations independently using official contact information.

The FBI has warned that fraudulent cryptocurrency recovery services may charge upfront fees and that private recovery companies cannot issue government seizure orders. The FTC also advises consumers to report refund and recovery scams.

8. What if you do not have the transaction hash?

Do not assume that you cannot report the scam. Provide whatever information you do have, such as the Bitcoin address, amount, approximate date, exchange or ATM used, screenshots, communications, website addresses, and the scammer's identifiers.

The FBI specifically states that victims should still submit a report if they do not have transaction information and should provide as much information as possible.

9. A simple Bitcoin scam report checklist

  • ☐ Bitcoin transaction ID/hash
  • ☐ Sending and receiving Bitcoin addresses
  • ☐ Amount of Bitcoin sent
  • ☐ Date, time, and time zone if known
  • ☐ Exchange, wallet, or Bitcoin ATM details
  • ☐ Scammer name, username, email, phone number, or other identifiers
  • ☐ Website and domain names
  • ☐ Messages, emails, screenshots, and receipts
  • ☐ Short timeline of what happened
  • ☐ Reports made to the exchange, ATM operator, police, cybercrime service, or regulator

Frequently asked questions

Can I report a Bitcoin scam if I do not know who the scammer really is?

Yes. Provide the identifiers you do have, such as usernames, phone numbers, email addresses, websites, Bitcoin addresses, transaction hashes, and communications.

Can reporting a Bitcoin scam guarantee that I will get my Bitcoin back?

No. A report does not guarantee recovery. Be especially cautious of anyone who promises guaranteed recovery in exchange for an upfront payment.

Should I contact the exchange after a Bitcoin scam?

Yes. If an exchange, wallet provider, or Bitcoin ATM was involved, contact the provider through its official channel as soon as possible and provide the transaction details.

What is the most important Bitcoin information to save?

Save the transaction hash, Bitcoin addresses, amount, date and time, and information about the service used to send the funds. Also preserve communications and other evidence explaining how the scam occurred.
