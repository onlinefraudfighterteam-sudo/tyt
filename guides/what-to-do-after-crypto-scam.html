What to Do After a Cryptocurrency Scam

If you believe you have lost cryptocurrency to a scam, the first priority is to preserve evidence and prevent further loss. The steps below are designed to help you organize the facts before contacting exchanges, financial institutions, platforms, regulators, or law enforcement.

1. Stop further payments

Do not send additional cryptocurrency because someone says a payment, tax, unlock fee, verification fee, or deposit is required to release your funds. Requests like these are common in recovery scams.

2. Preserve the evidence

Save transaction hashes, wallet addresses, screenshots, emails, chat messages, payment receipts, website addresses, usernames, phone numbers, and a timeline of events. Keep original files where possible.

3. Secure accounts you still control

Change compromised passwords, enable strong authentication, review active sessions, and contact the relevant exchange or financial institution if an account or payment method may have been compromised. Never share recovery codes or seed phrases.

4. Record the blockchain information

For each transfer, record the transaction ID or hash, network, sending address, receiving address, date, amount, and asset. This makes later review much easier.

5. Report the incident

Depending on the circumstances, consider reporting the fraud to the exchange involved, payment provider, relevant platform, law-enforcement agency, or other appropriate authority. Provide organized evidence rather than relying on a verbal description alone.

6. Be realistic about recovery

Blockchain transactions may be difficult or impossible to reverse. Tracing information can help establish the movement of funds, but it does not automatically identify a person or guarantee recovery.

Safety reminder: Never provide private keys, seed phrases, passwords, or authentication codes to someone claiming they can recover cryptocurrency. Recovery is never guaranteed.

Contact Online Fraud Fighter Team if you want to discuss the information you have. Providing more money is not a guarantee of recovery.
