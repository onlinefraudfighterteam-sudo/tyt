How to Report a Cryptocurrency Scam by Country

If you have lost cryptocurrency or money to a scam, report it to the appropriate authorities in your country and to the exchange, bank, payment provider, or platform involved. Reporting does not guarantee recovery, but it can preserve an official record and may help authorities connect related cases.

Important: Do not pay anyone who guarantees recovery of your cryptocurrency. Never give a supposed investigator, recovery agent, exchange employee, or government representative your seed phrase, private key, password, one-time code, or remote access.

Before you report

  • Save the transaction hash or transaction ID.
  • Record the sending and receiving wallet addresses.
  • Note the cryptocurrency, amount, date, and approximate time.
  • Save scammer usernames, email addresses, phone numbers, websites, app names, and social-media profiles.
  • Keep screenshots, messages, invoices, payment receipts, bank records, and a short timeline.
  • Contact the exchange, bank, card provider, or payment service through its official website or app if a payment can still be stopped or flagged.

United States

Report cryptocurrency scams to the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3). The FBI asks victims to provide cryptocurrency addresses, amounts and types of cryptocurrency, transaction hashes, dates and times, and other identifying information when available.

You can also report fraud to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC). Depending on the facts, the CFTC or SEC may also be relevant.

United Kingdom

Report a scam to Report Fraud. For suspected investment or cryptoasset firms or promotions within the FCA's remit, use the Financial Conduct Authority scam reporting guidance. If you are in Scotland, follow the FCA's current guidance for Police Scotland or Advice Direct Scotland.

Canada

Report fraud or cybercrime to your local police and to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC). The CAFC says its reporting system helps collect intelligence and support law-enforcement investigations, including international fraud.

Australia

Report scams through Scamwatch. If the incident is a cybercrime, you can also make a police report through ReportCyber. Scamwatch notes that a Scamwatch report itself is not an official police report.

European Union and Europe

There is no single public Europol complaint form for individual victims. Europol advises victims to contact their local or national police; authorities can involve Europol when international coordination is needed. If the scam involves an exchange or regulated financial service, also report it to the relevant financial regulator in your country.

Kenya

For a cyber incident in Kenya, use the National KE-CIRT/CC incident reporting service. For a criminal complaint or financial fraud, also contact local police or the appropriate Kenyan law-enforcement authority. Keep transaction hashes, wallet addresses, payment records, and scam communications.

South Africa

South African Police Service guidance says victims of suspected computer crime, identity theft, or commercial scams should report the matter at their local police station. Preserve digital evidence and give investigators the relevant transaction and communication details.

Nigeria

For financial and economic crimes in Nigeria, the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) provides a petition route. The EFCC says petitions should contain the facts of the complaint and supporting documents or sources of information. You can also report the crime to the appropriate local law-enforcement authority.

India

Use India's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal. It includes reporting routes for financial fraud and other cybercrime, including cryptocurrency-related complaints. For cyber financial fraud, the portal currently directs victims to call 1930 for immediate reporting.

If you live somewhere else

Start with your local or national police or cybercrime reporting authority. If the scammer or exchange is in another country, still report the crime where you live. INTERPOL explains that individuals should report international crimes to their local police; police agencies can coordinate internationally when appropriate.

Also report to the service you used

If you sent cryptocurrency through an exchange, wallet provider, bank, card, payment app, ATM, or other service, contact that provider through its official website or app. Give them the transaction hash, destination address, amount, date, and relevant account or payment reference. Do not use contact details supplied by the scammer.
