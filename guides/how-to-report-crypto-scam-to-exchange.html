How to Report a Crypto Scam to an Exchange

If a cryptocurrency exchange was used to buy, send, receive, or cash out funds connected with a scam, contacting the exchange quickly can create a record of the incident and may help the provider review the account or transaction. An exchange cannot guarantee that stolen cryptocurrency will be recovered, but a clear report with precise evidence is more useful than a general complaint.

Important: Contact the exchange only through its official website or app. Scammers sometimes impersonate exchange employees and ask for passwords, one-time codes, identification documents, or additional cryptocurrency. Never send extra funds to "unlock," "verify," "tax," or "recover" a transaction.

1. Stop the scam and secure your account

  • Stop sending additional cryptocurrency to the scammer.
  • If your exchange account may have been compromised, use the exchange's official security tools to change your password and secure the account.
  • Review recent logins, withdrawals, transfers, and account changes if the exchange provides those records.
  • Enable stronger account security, such as multi-factor authentication, using the exchange's official settings.
  • Do not give anyone your password, private key, wallet seed phrase, or one-time authentication code.

2. Use the exchange's official reporting channel

Find the exchange's support or fraud-reporting process by navigating to its official website or official mobile application yourself. Do not use a support link, phone number, or email address supplied by the person who scammed you.

Depending on the provider, the reporting option may be called a fraud report, security report, account compromise report, transaction dispute, abuse report, or support ticket. Follow the provider's current instructions.

Safety check: If someone contacts you claiming to be an exchange employee, do not rely on the contact information they provide. The FBI warns about scammers impersonating cryptocurrency exchange employees. Independently navigate to the exchange's official website and contact the provider through its published channel.

3. Tell the exchange exactly what happened

Keep the first report factual and organized. Explain that you believe the transaction or account activity is connected to fraud and provide the strongest identifiers you have.

Include transaction information

  • Transaction ID or transaction hash
  • Cryptocurrency type and amount
  • Sending and receiving wallet addresses, where available
  • Date and time of the transaction, including the time zone if known
  • Exchange account involved
  • Deposit, withdrawal, or transfer reference numbers shown by the exchange

Include information about the scam

  • How you first encountered the scammer
  • Names, usernames, phone numbers, email addresses, and other identifiers
  • Websites, domains, applications, or social-media profiles used
  • What the scammer told you and how you were instructed to make the payment
  • A short timeline of the events
  • Relevant screenshots, messages, emails, receipts, and account records

The FBI advises cryptocurrency scam victims to provide transaction details such as cryptocurrency addresses, amount and type, date and time, and transaction hash, along with information about communications, websites, applications, exchanges, and the timeline of the scam. citeturn0search1turn0search7

4. Ask the exchange what action is available

Ask the provider whether it can review the transaction or account activity and whether any action is available under its procedures. If the transaction was fraudulent, clearly state that and ask whether the provider can stop, freeze, flag, or reverse the transaction where its rules and circumstances allow.

Do not assume that an exchange can reverse a completed blockchain transaction. Cryptocurrency transfers may be difficult or impossible to reverse once confirmed. The FTC advises consumers who paid a scammer with cryptocurrency to contact the exchange or ATM operator immediately, explain that the transaction was fraudulent, and ask whether it can be reversed or the funds returned. citeturn0search6

5. Keep your support case information

After submitting a report, preserve the case number or ticket ID and copies of your correspondence with the exchange.

  • Save the support ticket number.
  • Save confirmation emails and messages.
  • Record the date and time you reported the incident.
  • Keep copies of documents or screenshots you submitted.
  • Record any instructions the exchange gives you.

6. Report the scam outside the exchange too

Reporting to an exchange does not replace reporting the underlying fraud to appropriate authorities. Depending on where you live, you may have separate reporting options through police, a national cybercrime service, a financial regulator, a consumer-protection agency, or another government authority.

United States

In the United States, cryptocurrency scam victims can report the fraud to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) and to the Federal Trade Commission. IC3 encourages victims to report even if they do not have every transaction detail. citeturn0search1turn0search7

Outside the United States

Use official government websites to identify the correct reporting service in your country. You may also need to notify local law enforcement or a financial or consumer-protection authority. Do not rely on contact details supplied by the scammer.

7. What if the scammer pretended to be the exchange?

Impersonation scams can look convincing. A scammer may claim that your exchange account is compromised, that a withdrawal must be cancelled, or that you need to move cryptocurrency to a "safe" wallet.

  • Do not click the scammer's links.
  • Do not provide login credentials or authentication codes.
  • Do not transfer cryptocurrency to a "safe" address supplied by an unsolicited caller or message.
  • Open the exchange's official website or app independently.
  • Report the impersonation to the real exchange and to appropriate authorities.

The FBI has specifically warned about criminals impersonating cryptocurrency exchange employees and recommends independently contacting the exchange through its official phone number or website. citeturn0search0

8. Be careful of recovery scams after reporting

Once a scam has been reported, another person may contact you claiming they can trace or recover the cryptocurrency. They may say they work for an exchange, law enforcement agency, law firm, blockchain investigation company, or recovery service.

  • Do not pay an upfront fee because someone promises guaranteed recovery.
  • Do not give a stranger your seed phrase, private key, password, or authentication codes.
  • Do not send more cryptocurrency to "release" recovered funds.
  • Verify organizations independently using official contact information.

The FBI warns about cryptocurrency recovery schemes and says private-sector recovery companies cannot issue seizure orders. citeturn0search4

9. Exchange reporting checklist

  • ☐ Official exchange website or app identified independently
  • ☐ Account secured if compromise is suspected
  • ☐ Transaction ID/hash saved
  • ☐ Cryptocurrency type and amount recorded
  • ☐ Wallet addresses recorded
  • ☐ Date and time recorded
  • ☐ Exchange reference or withdrawal/deposit ID saved
  • ☐ Scammer names, usernames, phone numbers, emails, and domains saved
  • ☐ Messages, screenshots, receipts, and other evidence preserved
  • ☐ Exchange support ticket/case number saved
  • ☐ Relevant government or law-enforcement report submitted

Frequently asked questions

Should I report a crypto scam to the exchange?

Yes, if the exchange was involved in sending, receiving, buying, selling, or holding the cryptocurrency connected with the scam. Contact the provider through its official channel and provide the transaction and fraud details you have.

Can an exchange reverse a cryptocurrency transaction?

It depends on the transaction, provider, and applicable procedures. Do not assume that a confirmed blockchain transfer can be reversed. Contact the exchange immediately and ask what action is available.

What information should I give the exchange?

Provide the transaction hash or ID, cryptocurrency type and amount, addresses, date and time, exchange account details, relevant support references, and evidence explaining how the scam occurred.

What if someone claiming to work for the exchange contacts me?

Do not trust the message merely because it uses the exchange's name or branding. Contact the exchange independently through its official website or app and never provide passwords or authentication codes to an unsolicited contact.
